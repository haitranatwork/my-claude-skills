# APA 7

Use these rules to write citations, References, and headings, and to check them in a draft you review. When you check the user's draft, report each problem in your review wherever these rules say to add a flag.

## In the text

- Cite as `(Author, Year)` or `Author (Year)`, plus the location when the source has one: `(Author, Year, p. 12)`.
- **Two authors.** Join the names with "and" in the sentence, and with "&" inside brackets and in References: `Author A and Author B (Year)` and `(Author A & Author B, Year)`.
- **Name the author or the work.** Refer to a cited author by surname ("Dordal explains," "Dordal adds") or name the work ("the book explains," "the book's example," "the video shows"). This keeps the focus on the source, and a pronoun such as "he," "she," or "they" for one author would guess the author's gender.
- **Page numbers.** Give a page number in every citation when the source has pages, for paraphrases too, not only quotes. Without pages, use a paragraph number (`para. 4`), a section name, or a timestamp for a video. A page number is copied from the source, never guessed: when it is missing, cite without it and add `[CHECK CITATION: ...]` to the evidence block.
- **Direct quotes** in the assignment text take quotation marks and a page number. Keep them short (under 40 words) and rare, and paraphrase when you can. Quotes in evidence blocks are notes for the user, not part of the assignment, so this rule does not cover them.
- **Paraphrase in new words.** Compare each cited sentence with the source text, and rewrite any sentence that keeps the source's wording or sentence pattern.
- **Cite what the user read.** An idea met only through the textbook takes a secondary citation, such as `(Goleman, 1995, as cited in [Textbook Author], [Year])`, and only the textbook goes in References.

## Web page dates

For a source at a URL, use only the date parts the page itself shows the reader, such as a byline or a "Published" or "Last updated" line:
- a full date gives (2024, March 5); a month and year give (2024, March); a year alone gives (2024);
- no date shown gives (n.d.) in References and (Author, n.d.) in the text.

Take the date only from what the reader sees. Page metadata (meta tags, structured data), the search result, the URL, and memory are all out, because the user and the grader cannot see them on the page to check. Use a "last updated" date for the content. A "last reviewed" date and the footer copyright year are not publish dates in APA 7. If the day and month order is unclear (05/03/2024), use the year only and add `[CHECK CITATION: ...]`.

## References

- End with a **References** section in APA 7 format: alphabetical by author surname, each entry with a hanging indent. Every in-text citation appears in References, and every reference is cited in the text.
- **Capitals.** Titles of articles, books, and web pages are in sentence case. Journal names are in title case.
- **Hanging indent.** In a Word file, set a real hanging indent on each entry. A Markdown file cannot show one, so remind the user to set it in the file they submit. The reminder goes in your notes to the user, never in the assignment itself.
- **DOI URLs.** When a source has a DOI, its entry ends with the DOI URL, `https://doi.org/10.xxxx/xxxxx`, with no full stop after it. The DOI URL takes the place of `doi:10.xxxx/xxxxx`, a bare number, the publisher's page, or a course site link, because it keeps working when a publisher moves the page. Copy the DOI from the source: the article's first page, or the page you opened. If a journal article shows none, look on the publisher's page or in Crossref, and open the DOI URL to check that it leads to the same title and authors. If you cannot confirm one, leave it out and add `[CHECK CITATION: DOI not found]` to the first evidence entry for that source. A source with no DOI keeps its usual APA 7 form.
- **Every detail comes from the source.** Author names, years, titles, page numbers, and DOIs are copied from what you opened, never written from memory. When a detail is missing, leave it out and add `[CHECK CITATION: ...]` to the first evidence entry for that source.

## Title and headings

- The title is in title case, and it names the topic of the assignment.
- **Level 1 headings** are centered, bold, and in title case. **Level 2 headings** are flush left, bold, and in title case.
- In a Markdown file, write the title as a `#` heading, Level 1 as `##`, and Level 2 as `###`. The user sets the centering and the bold in the file they submit.
