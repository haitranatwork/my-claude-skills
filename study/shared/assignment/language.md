# Language

The user writes at CEFR B1 level, and everything written in the user's voice must read as if the user wrote it. Plain language wins over every other goal, the top band included: depth comes from ideas, not from bigger words.

## Words

- Use common, everyday words at B1 level or below: show, use, help, important, problem, idea, also, but, because, however.
- **B2 words** are welcome where they make a sentence clearer or more exact than the B1 wording, such as "significant" for "big and important." Keep them to 10% of the words in the assignment text, or fewer.
- **Technical terms from the course** stay as the course writes them, at any level, and are outside the 10%. Every other word above B2 gets a plainer replacement.
- Start each sentence with its point. Filler openings such as "It is important to note that," "In today's society," or "Since the dawn of time" add nothing.
- Replace any word that sounds like AI writing with a plain one. Check for these in particular: delve, navigate, leverage, underscore, tapestry, multifaceted, paradigm, intricate, crucial, pivotal, foster.

## Sentences

- **Length:** an average of 20 to 25 words per sentence, and never more than 30 words. Mix shorter and longer sentences.
- **Shape:** one clause, or two clauses joined by and, but, because, so, when, or if. One idea per sentence, and one topic per paragraph.
- **Voice:** active. Use the passive only when the actor does not matter.

## Tone

An assignment is more formal than a forum post, and just as plain:
- **Full forms:** "do not," "it is," and "cannot," where speech would shorten them.
- **Statements:** each point is a statement. A question to the reader appears only when the assignment asks for one.
- **Hedges:** an uncertain claim takes "may," "suggests," or "one possible reason."
- **First person** for the user's own experience and view ("in my team," "I think"), and all through a reflection paper. Everywhere else, the subject of the sentence is the idea, the source, or the evidence.

## Paragraphs and signposting

Write in prose paragraphs, and use a list or a table only when the instructions ask for one. Open each body paragraph with its topic sentence. Signpost the argument so the grader can follow it: the introduction names the order of the sections, and linking words show how each paragraph relates to the one before, such as however, in contrast, for example, as a result, and for this reason. Vary how paragraphs open and how long they are.

## Dashes

Join ideas with a full stop, a comma, a colon, or brackets. Write ranges in sentences with words ("from 2021 to 2023", "20 to 25 words"). In citations and References, write page ranges with a hyphen (pp. 12-18). Hyphens inside words (well-known, two-way) are fine.

New text never uses an en dash (–), an em dash (—), or a hyphen with spaces around it ( - ). These appear only inside text copied from a source that has them. The user's own writing has no dashes, so a dash does not sound like the user.

**Dash check before delivering.** Search the whole output for – and —. Keep each one only if it comes from copied text that has it in the original. Rewrite every other one.

## Exact copying

Text copied from a source or from the user keeps every character as it is: quotes, titles, names, URLs, DOIs, code, commands, software output, and the user's own sentences. A hyphen stays a hyphen, and no other character gets "cleaned up". A changed character breaks things: a quote can no longer be found with Ctrl+F, a URL, DOI, or piece of code stops working, and the user's text changes without their say.

## Language check

When the output includes a language check, it covers the assignment text (never the evidence blocks) and reports:
- the average sentence length, against the target of 20 to 25 words;
- every sentence over 30 words, with a way to split it;
- the B2 words used, and their share of the text against the 10% limit;
- every word above B2 that is not a technical term from the course, with a plain replacement;
- every contraction and every question to the reader, with the full form or the statement;
- the result of the dash check.
