# Reading the rubric

Every assignment aims at the **top band** of the rubric, not a pass. The gap between the band below and the top band is almost never more words or bigger words. It is usually one verb: *describe* becomes *analyze*, *mention a source* becomes *integrate sources*, *summarize* becomes *evaluate and apply*. Find that verb for each criterion. Top-band thinking still uses the plain language of `language.md`: a bigger word adds no depth, and it stops sounding like the user.

## Decode it

1. List every criterion and its points or weight.
2. Take the wording of each criterion's **top band** (the "Exemplary," "Excellent," "Distinguished," or full-points column). That wording is the specification.
3. **Find the delta.** Compare the top band with the band directly below it. The words that appear only in the top band are what the grader actually checks. If the band below says "uses sources" and the top band says "integrates multiple sources," the delta is not more citations: it is at least one place where two sources are put in relation. Write down the delta for each criterion. An assignment earns the top band only when each delta is visible enough to point at.
4. **Make each top-band descriptor concrete:** one thing the assignment must contain, such as a sentence, a paragraph, or a citation, and the section where it will sit. "Demonstrates critical analysis" is not concrete; "one paragraph in the second section that names a condition where the concept fails, with a citation" is. Common rubric verbs:

| Top-band verb | What the assignment must contain |
|---|---|
| analyze | one concept broken into parts or causes, and how they relate |
| evaluate / critique | two ideas or sources compared, the strengths and limits of each, then a judgment with a stated reason |
| apply / connect to practice | the concept used on a specific real case (the user's work, a real event), with the outcome or the lesson stated |
| synthesize / integrate | two sources in one paragraph that support a single point: they agree, one extends the other, or they conflict, and the text says which |
| compare / contrast | both sides named, plus one sentence on which fits when |
| justify / support | every claim followed by a reason, an example, or a citation |
| insight / originality | one step past the sources: a limit they do not mention, a condition where the claim fails, or what the idea means in practice |
| coherent argument / organization | a thesis in the introduction, a topic sentence in each paragraph that links back to it, and linking words between paragraphs |
| accurate referencing | APA 7 format with no errors, and citations and References that match both ways |

5. **Separate what the assignment can earn from what it cannot.** Submitting by the deadline, and anything else graded outside the text, is earned outside the assignment. These are the "on you" criteria.
6. **Budget words by points.** A criterion worth 40% of the grade gets about 40% of the words. When critical thinking outweighs summary, the critical paragraphs are the longest ones.

## Special cases

- **No bands** (a checklist, or one description per criterion): treat every listed requirement as top-band wording, and skip the delta.
- **No rubric:** ask the user for it once, because the top band cannot be targeted blind. If the user answers that there is none, use `top-band-standard.md`, and say at the top of the output that this default standard was used.
