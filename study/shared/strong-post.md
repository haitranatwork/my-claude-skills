# A strong initial post

## What the grader looks for

- **A clear position** in the first one or two sentences. A post that builds up to its answer over three paragraphs reads as unfocused.
- **Every part of the question answered**, each in the order the question asks. Top marks on content are usually lost to a missed part, not to weak thinking.
- **Course material named specifically:** the concept, the author, or the study, never a vague "the readings showed."
- **Critical thinking:** a question asked, two ideas compared, or a concept applied to a new example. Agreeing with the source is not enough.
- **Specific critique.** A limit that fits any theory ("may not apply to all cultures," "more research is needed") does not count. Name the specific condition where the idea fails.
- **Evidence after each claim:** a reason, an example, or a citation.
- **Honest uncertainty.** "I am not sure if X, because..." is stronger than overclaiming.
- **A real closing question:** open, not yes/no, not rhetorical, and not one the post already answered.

## Structure

Cover all four parts, and keep it natural:
1. **Position:** the answer to the question in the first one or two sentences.
2. **Support:** two or three short paragraphs that answer the parts of the question, each with evidence and citations. Use at least two sources, and at least once put two of them together: they agree, one extends the other, or they pull in different directions. One source per paragraph, each sitting alone, reads as summary and stays in the middle band.
3. **Critical angle:** one paragraph the course material does not hand you: a condition where the concept does not hold, a real case from the user's work or study, a tension between two sources, or a trade-off the material skips. It carries a citation too, since a supported opinion scores above an unsupported one.
4. **Open question:** one genuine question for classmates.

## Title

The title goes on the first line, before the post: about 4 to 10 plain words, specific to the topic. It can hint at the position or the angle instead of repeating the question.

## The depth ladder

Every claim sits on one of three levels, and the top band lives on level 3:
- **Level 1, repeat:** "Scarbrough (2023) lists five conflict styles."
- **Level 2, explain:** "Collaboration takes more time, because both sides have to agree on the problem first."
- **Level 3, apply and judge:** "In my team, collaboration worked for the deadline conflict. It would have failed if we had needed a decision the same day. The style has to fit the time available, not only the relationship."

A post whose claims mostly stop at level 1 or 2 is not top band yet. The critical-angle paragraph must reach level 3; if it only explains, it is a support paragraph under the wrong label.

## Audience and originality

The readers are classmates and the instructor, who have studied the same course material. The post skips the basics and includes at least one angle that is not obvious from the course material.

## Why good posts miss the top band

- One part of a multi-part question is left unanswered.
- Sources are summarized instead of used as evidence for the user's own claim.
- The critical paragraph has no citation.
- The closing question is yes/no, or the post already answered it.
- References and in-text citations do not match.
- The word count falls outside the range.
