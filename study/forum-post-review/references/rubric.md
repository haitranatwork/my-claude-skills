# Reading the rubric

Every initial post aims at the **top band** of the rubric, not a pass. The gap between the band below and the top band is almost never more words or bigger words. It is usually one verb: *describe* becomes *analyze*, *mention a source* becomes *integrate sources*, *summarize* becomes *evaluate and apply*. Find that verb for each criterion. Top-band thinking still uses the plain language of `language.md`: a bigger word adds no depth, and it stops sounding like the user.

## Decode it

1. List every criterion and its points or weight.
2. Take the wording of each criterion's **top band** (the "Exemplary," "Excellent," "Distinguished," or full-points column). That wording is the specification.
3. **Find the delta.** Compare the top band with the band directly below it. The words that appear only in the top band are what the grader actually checks. If the band below says "uses sources" and the top band says "integrates multiple sources," the delta is not more citations: it is at least one place where two sources are put in relation. Write down the delta for each criterion. A post earns the top band only when each delta is visible enough to point at.
4. **Make each top-band descriptor concrete:** one thing the post must contain, such as a sentence, a paragraph, a citation, or a question. "Demonstrates critical analysis" is not concrete; "one paragraph that names a condition where the concept fails, with a citation" is. Common rubric verbs:

| Top-band verb | What the post must contain |
|---|---|
| analyze | one concept broken into parts or causes, and how they relate |
| evaluate / critique | a judgment with a stated reason: what works, what does not, and under which condition |
| apply | the concept used on a specific new case (the user's work, a real event), not the textbook example |
| synthesize / integrate | two sources in one paragraph: they agree, one extends the other, or they conflict, and the post says which |
| compare / contrast | both sides named, plus one sentence on which fits when |
| justify / support | every claim followed by a reason, an example, or a citation |
| stimulate discussion | a closing question a classmate can actually answer with the course material |
| connect to practice / real world | one concrete example from the user's own work or life, tied back to a named concept |

5. **Separate what the post can earn from what it cannot.** Timeliness, posting by a deadline, the number of replies, and participation across the week are earned outside the post. These are the "on you" criteria.
6. **Budget words by points.** A criterion worth 40% of the grade gets about 40% of the words. When critical thinking outweighs summary, the critical paragraphs are the longest ones.

## Special cases

- **No bands** (a checklist, or one description per criterion): treat every listed requirement as top-band wording, and skip the delta.
- **No rubric:** ask the user for it once, because the top band cannot be targeted blind. If there is none, use `top-band-standard.md`, and say at the top of the output that this default standard was used.
